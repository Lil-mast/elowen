*(Or: Why I'm Trading Debugging Logs for Digital Topsoil)*

**3 AM. Again.**
My screen glows like a firefly in a jar, another backend loop collapsed
into spaghetti, another cascade of `undefined` errors bleeding across
the console. I'd scribbled fixes in leather notebooks for years, ink
bleeding into margins like roots seeking water. But last night, as I
traced a memory leak through 200 lines of Python, it hit me: I wasn't
just fixing code. I was cultivating something.

So I'm planting a garden. Not in soil, but in silicon and silence.

This isn't a blog. It's not a newsletter. It's a living digital
ecosystem, where half-baked AI experiments root beside philosophical
ramblings, where Rust errors compost into clarity, and where every
failed prototype becomes fertile ground for what grows next.

Why a garden? Because tech isn't built — it grows.

- **[[seeds|Seeds]]**: A single GPT prompt. A messy Jupyter notebook.
  The spark when a neural net almost understands poetry. 😅
- **[[weeds|Weeds]]**: My own imposter syndrome. The fear of posting
  "not-ready" work. The noise of hot takes drowning real insight.
- **[[harvest|Harvest]]**: Not followers or claps — but connections.
  When someone replies, "This is exactly what I was wrestling with,"
  we've cross-pollinated.

I used to think tech thrived in sterile labs. Now I know: the most
resilient ideas grow wild. They twist through the cracks of "best
practices," adapt to the chaos of real human needs, and bloom in the
unlikeliest places — like a chatbot that learns empathy from
debugging logs, or a CSS animation that mirrors how neurons fire.

This garden will be imperfect. Intentionally.

Welcome to the overgrowth. Where code breathes, thoughts take root,
and every failed commit is just compost for tomorrow's breakthrough.

Let's get our hands dirty.

~ Taz
