*(Or: What a Plant Taught Me About Staying Alive)*

**Every morning, before code, before coffee, I water Elowen.**

Same cup. Same corner of light. Some mornings she looks like nothing
happened overnight. Some mornings there's a new leaf, small and
stubborn, reaching toward whatever light it can find. I never know
which morning I'm going to get. I just show up with the water anyway.

Elowen means new beginnings. I didn't pick the name for decoration;
I picked it because I needed a daily reminder standing in a pot on my
desk, silent, doing the one thing it knows how to do: grow, slowly,
whether or not anyone's watching.

Why does a plant on a desk matter to a career? Because isn't that the
whole question underneath everything, the pull requests, the
half-finished projects, the mornings you don't feel like showing up?
What are you actually building, if not yourself, one unglamorous day
at a time? How many of us mistake stillness for failure, when it's
just the part of growth that doesn't make a good commit message?

Elowen doesn't rush. She doesn't compare herself to the plant in the
next room. She just keeps her roots down and her leaves up, and one
day you look and she's twice the size she was, and you can't even say
exactly when it happened.

Purpose works the same way, I think. You don't arrive at it; you
water it. Daily. Quietly. Especially on the mornings it doesn't look
like anything is happening at all.

So strive. Not for the applause, not for the finished thing: strive
to live the kind of life that survives its own bad mornings, the way
Elowen survives mine.

*"Welcome to the digital garden."*

~ Taz
