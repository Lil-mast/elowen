*Enough*

**The calendar just turned, and it doesn't ask permission.**

No ceremony. No warning shot. Just a new number on the page, and
underneath it, the same question that shows up every time a month
dies and another one starts: is there still time? Is a second chance
even still on the table, or did the window already close somewhere
back in March, in April, on some ordinary Tuesday you weren't paying
attention to?

Here's what nobody tells you about second chances: they don't arrive
announced. There's no notification. The chance was just quietly
there the whole time, in the shape of today, waiting to see if you'd
notice it before it became yesterday too.

I keep coming back to sunflowers. Not the version of them we've
flattened into a metaphor for cheerfulness, the actual biology of
them. A young sunflower spends its early life in motion. All day, it
tracks the sun east to west, bending to follow the light, and
overnight it quietly resets, swings back east, so it's ready to
catch the first light of the next morning all over again. That
movement is called heliotropism. It's not decorative. It's the plant
doing the most important work of its life: orienting itself toward
what will make it grow, over and over, every single day, without
complaint.

But here's the part that actually matters. As the sunflower matures,
something changes. It stops swinging back and forth. It picks a
direction - east - and it stays there. Fixed. Facing the sunrise for
good. And that's not the plant giving up on the sun. That's the plant
having found where the growth actually happens, and choosing to stop
wasting motion chasing anything else. The flowers that fix east end
up catching more morning warmth, growing heavier, holding more seed,
than the ones still swinging. Commitment, it turns out, outperforms
constant searching.

So, is it worth it? Is five months, or whatever's left of this year,
enough to turn something around? I think that's the wrong question,
honestly. The sunflower doesn't ask if there's enough sunlight left
in the day to matter. It just turns. Every morning it can. That's the
whole strategy. Not certainty that it'll be enough, just showing up
facing the right direction, on repeat, until the facing becomes fixed.

What would you stop swinging toward, if you picked your east right
now?

Five months is longer than you think. People have turned their whole
life around in less. So can you.

Digital garden Grows.

~ Taz
