*(Or: What Grief Has in Common With a Broken Pointer)*

**There is a particular cruelty in muscle memory.**

The hand still reaches for the phone at the hour it used to buzz. The
mind still drafts the sentence it meant to say tonight, still turns
toward the place a person used to occupy, the way a compass needle
keeps swinging north even after you've left the room the magnet was
in. Nobody warns you that heartbreak isn't a single wound. It's a
thousand small, involuntary reachings toward something that is no
longer there to be reached.

In the systems I build, there's a fault with an almost merciless
precision to its name: a null reference. A pointer that once held the
address of something real, something allocated, something you could
call upon and trust to answer, and then, without ceremony, the thing
it pointed to is gone. The pointer itself doesn't know that yet. It
still holds the shape of the address. It still reaches. And when it
reaches, the whole program halts, violently, on contact with an
absence it wasn't built to survive.

That is, with unflinching accuracy, what this feels like. Not the
absence alone; absence, given enough time, becomes bearable, even
familiar. It's the reaching that undoes you. The instinctive,
uncorrected habit of a heart still addressed to somewhere it can no
longer arrive.

I will not perform healing I have not yet arrived at. I will not
dress this grief in language borrowed from people who have already
crossed to the other side of it. Some sorrows deserve to be felt in
their full weight before they are permitted to become wisdom, and I
suspect this is one of them. So let it be sad. Let the ache be exactly
as large as it is, unshrunk, unexplained away, ungarnished with
premature gratitude. Despite everything I have carried, everything I
have already survived, I am allowed this: to simply hurt, honestly,
without apologizing for the size of it.

But even here, even at the bottom of it, there is a discipline I
refuse to surrender. A well-designed system, faced with a fault it
cannot immediately fix, does not corrupt everything around it out of
spite. It degrades gracefully. It protects what remains, even while
grieving what was lost. And so must I. Whatever this pain wants to
rewrite in me, whatever bitterness it whispers would be justified, I
will not let it recompile who I am underneath it. I will still be
patient with people who did not cause this. I will still forgive,
not because the debt is small, but because I refuse to let sorrow
author my character for me.

Do not let pain rewrite obedience and forgiveness.

~ Taz
