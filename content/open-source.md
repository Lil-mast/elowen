*(Or: Why Nobody Grows Alone in a Sealed Room)*

**A strawberry plant doesn't grow taller to survive. It sends runners.**

Thin green lines reaching out along the soil, away from the mother
plant, until one touches down somewhere new and roots. Within weeks
that runner is its own plant entirely, independent, fruiting,
sending out runners of its own. The original plant didn't grow by
hoarding its energy inward. It grew by giving pieces of itself away
and trusting the ground to receive them.

I've been chasing bounties on open-source projects lately, small
fixes and features for codebases I didn't build and don't own, and
somewhere in the middle of one of those pull requests it hit me: this
is the fastest I've grown in months. Not from the project I control
completely, the one where I make every decision alone. From the ones
where I had to read someone else's architecture, respect conventions
I didn't invent, and submit a change knowing a stranger would review
it, question it, maybe reject it outright. That friction is the whole
mechanism. A pull request only becomes a pull request because it's
offered into something bigger than the person who wrote it.

Life runs on the same current, if you let it. The version of me that
only stays inside familiar rooms, with familiar people, doing
familiar things, grows the way a plant grows in a sealed container:
slowly, and only up to the size of the pot. But the version of me
that shows up to volunteer somewhere he wasn't required to be, that
sits with a sick friend when there's nothing productive to say, that
says yes to being useful in a room he could have easily skipped,
that version keeps rooting into new ground, and keeps growing past
whatever size he thought was his ceiling.

A few of the runners worth sending out, if you're looking for where
to start:

- **Contribute somewhere you don't get credit**: - an open-source
  issue, a task nobody's watching, a favor with no audience.
- **Show up where someone is struggling**: - a hospital bed, a hard
  week, a friend who just needs a body in the room, not a solution.
- **Give your time to something structural**: - church, community,
  whatever holds people together where you live, not because it's
  owed, but because it's how the whole system stays alive.

None of this costs you your personality. If anything, it's the
opposite of erosion, every runner that roots somewhere new builds
something inside you that staying contained never could. Confidence
that isn't theoretical. Character that's been tested against real
people instead of just your own opinion of yourself.

And here's the part I have to keep relearning: not everything worth
contributing happens on a screen. Some of the best growth I've had
this year happened away from a keyboard entirely; outside, hands
busy, phone forgotten in another room. Detox from the glow
occasionally. The runners don't only grow through Wi-Fi.

So be adventurous. Say yes to the room you'd normally skip. Send the
runner out, root it in someone else's soil, and trust that whatever
grows there belongs to you too. The lesson underneath all of it is
simple, even if living it isn't: contribute to society. Not because
it's expected of you, but because it's the only way any of us
actually grow past the size of our own container.

We Thrive in The digital garden.

~ Taz
