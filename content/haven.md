*(Or: What a Codebase Taught Me About Belonging)*

**An acacia doesn't grow tall to be admired. It grows wide, so
something else can stand underneath it.**

Drive through most of this country and you'll see them scattered
across the plains, flat-canopied, deliberate, like the tree itself
decided height was never the point. Shade was the point. A place for
something else to rest. I think that's the first thing I actually
understood about my people, not that they were impressive, though
some of them are, but that they were built the same way. Wide enough
to stand under. Present enough that you'd notice if the shade
disappeared.

I know my people by a simple test now: not who claps loudest when
things go right, but who's still there, quietly present, when
they've gone wrong. That's the class I belong to. Not a status, not a
circle defined by achievement or proximity to something impressive.
A haven-class. People who exist, structurally, to hold space for
someone else.

Here's the part that took me longer to see, and it came to me, of
all places, through code. In object-oriented programming there's a
concept called polymorphism, the idea that different classes,
built entirely differently under the hood, storing different data,
solving different problems, can still respond to the same call in
their own way and produce something that works together. A circle
and a square are nothing alike internally. Ask them both to `draw()`,
and each does it faithfully, in its own shape, and the program
doesn't care that they're different; it just cares that when called
on, each one shows up as itself, reliably.

That's my people, when I actually look at them clearly. We don't fit
neatly into the same file. Some of them speak in scripture, some in
sarcasm, some in silence that says more than either. Some show up
with chapos, some with brutal honesty, some by simply refusing to
leave the room when things get hard. Different classes entirely. Hard
to imagine on paper how they'd belong in the same structure. And yet,  called on, when it actually matters, they all respond the same
way. They show up. Different implementations of the exact same
promise.

Life has a way of asking harder and harder questions of the people
around you, the way a program asks more of its objects the longer it
runs. Some relationships buckle under that. Mine, the ones that
matter, haven't. Not because they're identical to me, or even
particularly similar. Because whatever they're each built from,
whatever their own private architecture looks like, they were
written to answer the same call: be there.

So when life gets difficult, and it will, and some days it already
has, I don't brace for it alone. I know exactly who answers when
called. Different people, different shapes, different ways of
loving, all somehow returning the same result: presence.

That's the haven. Not a place. A class of people who show up shaped
however they need to, so I never have to stand in the open by
myself.

There is growth in the digital garden.

~ Taz
