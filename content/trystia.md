*(Or: When the Retry Feels Heavier Than the Failure)*

**Some days the weight is not in the work. It is in lifting your own hands.**

I missed the deadline. Not by much, and not for lack of caring. The
day simply arrived before I did.

Then the email came, polite and clean. *We have decided to move
forward with other candidates.* Four careful lines, for something I
carried for weeks.

Now I sit here, tired in a way sleep does not reach. The tasks are
still waiting. I look at them the way you look at a shoreline from far
out in the water.

This is the season of [[weeds]]. Not a dramatic collapse, just a slow
overgrowth. One unanswered message. One unopened file. One more day of
telling myself tomorrow. Nothing announces it. It spreads quietly,
until the garden I meant to tend is mostly the thing choking it.

And the hardest part is how alone it is. You go deep, deeper than
anyone nearby can see, and from the surface you look fine. People wave
from the shore. They cannot tell how far down the water goes, and you
stop trying to explain. What would you even say? That you are not sad
exactly, only heavy? That trying again feels bigger than the failure
itself?

In the systems I build, when a request fails, the program does not
quit. It waits, then tries again. Waits a little longer, then tries
again. The pause between attempts keeps growing, but the attempts
never stop. It is called exponential backoff, and I have always
admired how it treats failure: not as a verdict, only as a moment that
needs more room before the next try.

I want to be that gentle with myself. A longer pause is not the same as
giving up. Rest is not the end of the story, even when it feels like
the whole book.

A dandelion does not wait for good soil or kind weather. It finds the
crack in the concrete and comes up anyway, small and unremarkable and
completely alive.

I am not asking myself to be fine today. Only to be honest about where
I stand, because that is where every road out begins.

*To know where you are going, you must know where you are now.*

~ Taz
